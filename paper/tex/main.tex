\begin{abstract}
Provenance tooling can record what an agentic AI system did; it cannot record why. We argue
that this gap is structural: the facts a decision used may cross a step boundary, but the rule
that combined them into a verdict is a local computation, and no callback, span or post-hoc
query can retrieve what was never captured. We make the argument measurable with a reflexive
multi-agent demonstrator --- four operational agents emitting typed W3C PROV about themselves
and two meta agents governing that output --- and a deliberately generous callback-level
baseline over the same run. The baseline reconstructs every activity, all 324 relations and
every verdict, and sees 61\% of the facts the operational justifications cite, but none of the
values the agents computed in order to decide. We publish the \texttt{yp:} vocabulary as an
OWL ontology with SHACL shapes, and measure the cost: building the record is inside every
operational agent's run-to-run variance, justifications are 6.5\% of the records' bytes, and
measured CPU energy per session shows no resolvable difference.
\end{abstract}

provenance, W3C PROV, agentic AI, explainability, multi-agent systems, SHACL

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:1}

Scientific computing infrastructure is acquiring autonomous agents. They summarise runs, select hyperparameters, decide what to archive, and increasingly act on those decisions without a human in the loop. The provenance community has a mature answer to \emph{what} such a system did: W3C PROV~\cite{prov-dm} describes activities, entities and the lineage between them, and tooling to capture that lineage automatically is now routine.

It has almost no answer to \emph{why}.

When a storage agent declines to archive a record, when a summariser rejects its own output, when a compliance agent fails a colleague's record --- an operator, an auditor, and increasingly a regulator need the reason, not the outcome. The EU AI Act~\cite{euaiact}'s transparency obligations are written in the language of explanation rather than of logging. A record that says a decision was taken, and when, and by which component, and still cannot say why, does not meet that bar.

Our claim is that this gap is \textbf{structural rather than a matter of insufficient tooling}:

\begin{quote}
The rationale for a decision is a local variable at the moment of decision. A callback fires after the function returns; a span wraps the call; a query reads the store afterwards. Each may see the facts the decision used. None of them can retrieve the rule that combined those facts into a verdict, because that rule was never a value anything passed.
\end{quote}

We call this \textbf{capture, not retrieve}. The corollary is uncomfortable for the observability-first approach that dominates agent tooling: no amount of additional collection closes the gap, because the information is not in the span. Behavioural provenance therefore has an irreducible explicit component --- instrumentation a developer must write, at the point of decision, that no framework can supply.

\textbf{C1 --- Capture, not retrieve.} A structural argument supported by an A-versus-B comparison over the \emph{same agents in the same run}. We build the callback-level baseline ourselves and make it as strong as we can: it reconstructs 96 of 96 activities, all 324 relations and 24 of 24 verdicts, and its output is accepted by the reference W3C PROV parser. It recovers no justification verbatim, and we do not rest on that. Searched fact by fact, it saw 61\% of what the eight operational justifications cite --- and none of the values the agents computed in order to decide (Section~\ref{sec:6.2}).

\textbf{C2 --- A reflexive multi-agent provenance architecture.} Four operational agents emit typed PROV about themselves; two meta agents consume \emph{that} and emit PROV in turn. Containerised, run over a real message bus in CI, and governing its own governance layer to a depth we state exactly (Section~\ref{sec:5.1}).

\textbf{C3 --- The \texttt{yp:\allowbreak{}} vocabulary and a six-step lifecycle}, published as an OWL ontology with SHACL~\cite{shacl} shapes, instantiated by four heterogeneous agents --- deterministic, LLM-backed and hybrid --- and governed uniformly against the declared shapes (Section~\ref{sec:3}).

We also measure the cost, because an argument that explicit instrumentation is \emph{necessary} is worth little if it is expensive. Building the record, justification included, moves no operational agent's step by more than its own run-to-run variance, on either of two occasions; the justifications are 6.5\% of the records' bytes. What costs is writing the record to disk, and that is a deployment decision the library exposes (Section~\ref{sec:6.5}). Measured CPU energy per session shows no resolvable difference either (Section~\ref{sec:6.3}).

\section{Background and related work}\label{sec:2}

\textbf{W3C PROV} models provenance as three classes --- \texttt{prov:\allowbreak{}Entity}, \texttt{prov:\allowbreak{}Activity}, \texttt{prov:\allowbreak{}Agent} --- and a small set of relations between them. Its deliberate domain-neutrality is why it says nothing about rationale: PROV describes \emph{what was derived from what, by whom}, and a justification is none of those things. It is a property of an activity that no relation can express, which is why an extension vocabulary is needed rather than a modelling convention. This work sits in the \textbf{yProv} family, which instruments machine-learning runs (yProv4ML~\cite{yprov4ml}) and workflows (yProv4WFs) with content-addressed storage for large payloads; our demonstrator consumes real yProv4ML records, so the agents are exercised against provenance not written for them.

Agent frameworks differ in their abstractions but converge on a recurring shape, which we take as the unit of instrumentation, in three phases: \emph{ingestion and scoping} (S1 receive, S2 plan), \emph{execution} (S3 act --- the model call, the tool call, the write), and \emph{governance and reflexion} (S4 observe, \textbf{S5 decide}, S6 respond). S5 is the step this paper is about, and the only one whose content an external observer cannot reconstruct.

\textbf{PROV-AGENT~\cite{provagent}} (Souza et al.) is the closest prior work: it extends PROV for agentic systems and treats agents as first-class provenance subjects, but models data as untyped \texttt{DomainData} and carries no decision rationale --- it records a richer \emph{what}, and the \emph{why} remains outside the model. Work on post-hoc reasoning reconstruction (AER; Vispute et al.~\cite{aer}) formalises a result we rely on: \textbf{a reasoning trace cannot in general be identified from inputs and outputs alone}, because many distinct reasoning processes are consistent with the same observable behaviour. Where that literature treats this as a limit on explanation, we treat it as a design requirement. Meanwhile OpenTelemetry's GenAI conventions~\cite{otel-genai}, OpenLLMetry~\cite{openllmetry}, Langfuse~\cite{langfuse} and LangSmith~\cite{langsmith} have effectively solved automatic collection of the \emph{what}; none models W3C PROV and none captures rationale, because they are built for debugging and cost attribution. \textbf{Our position is to ride that layer rather than compete with it.} The planning/tool-use/reflection vocabulary of the applied agent literature supplies our lifecycle; we add nothing to it, we instrument it.

\section{The \texttt{yp:\allowbreak{}} vocabulary}\label{sec:3}

Published as an OWL ontology (\texttt{vocab/\allowbreak{}yprov4agents.\allowbreak{}ttl}, 86 terms) with SHACL shapes (\texttt{vocab/\allowbreak{}shapes.\allowbreak{}ttl}, 16 named constraints). Two rules governed its contents. \textbf{Only terms the system emits}: the inventory was taken from frozen archives of actual runs rather than from design documents, and a test enforces it in both directions, with terms the library offers but the demonstrator does not exercise enumerated explicitly rather than left for a reader to discover. \textbf{Standard terms wherever one exists}: all six lineage relations are PROV's, and \texttt{yp:\allowbreak{}} adds exactly two things PROV has no term for --- the typed steps of a decision lifecycle, and the reasoning behind a decision. Eleven lifecycle activity classes specialise \texttt{prov:\allowbreak{}Activity}, including \textbf{\texttt{Decision}} and two governance subclasses; five entity classes specialise \texttt{prov:\allowbreak{}Entity}, including \texttt{QualityEntity}, the outcome of a check made an entity rather than a flag so a decision can declare it was \emph{derived from} the finding it rests on.

\textbf{\texttt{yp:\allowbreak{}justification}} is one \texttt{owl:\allowbreak{}DatatypeProperty} with \texttt{rdfs:\allowbreak{}domain yp:\allowbreak{}DecisionActivity}. Alongside it sit \texttt{yp:\allowbreak{}verdict}, \texttt{yp:\allowbreak{}confidence\_\allowbreak{}score}, and \texttt{yp:\allowbreak{}escalation\_\allowbreak{}reason}. The asymmetry between verdict and justification is worth stating, because our own measurements surfaced it. A verdict is a short token from a fixed vocabulary, and a determined observer can sometimes recover one: our baseline recovers 24 of 24 by scraping the labels developers wrote them into. A justification combines evidence by a rule the step never returned to anyone, and no labelling convention exposes the rule. \textbf{The conclusion is sometimes recoverable, and often the facts are; the rule that joined them is not.}

\textbf{Shapes: declaring the standard.} A compliance agent that enforces a structural standard in hand-written predicates has made that standard a property of one implementation. \texttt{vocab/\allowbreak{}shapes.\allowbreak{}ttl} declares it instead: every \texttt{yp:\allowbreak{}DecisionActivity} carries a non-empty justification and a verdict; every lifecycle activity is dated, ended and attributed; every \texttt{yp:\allowbreak{}pid} is accompanied by a \texttt{sha256:\allowbreak{}} hash; every record declares its granularity and persistence; and every agent decides something --- a constraint we lacked until deleting a record's decision left it conforming, since a shape targeting a class is silent when no node of the class exists. A consumer can validate with any SHACL engine without running our agent, and the agent's runtime check, a second implementation of the standard, is tested \emph{against} the declaration. That test has found them disagreeing on whitespace justifications (plain, and as PROV-JSON typed literals), untyped or mistyped steps, and the deleted decision. \textbf{The standard is declared once, and each implementation is tested against it rather than trusted to match it.} One difference is deliberate and pinned: the agent requires a decision to state its type, which the shapes infer from \texttt{rdfs:\allowbreak{}domain}. The shapes are themselves tested for falsifiability: twelve mutations of a conforming record each produce the objection the corresponding shape exists to produce.

\section{Instrumentation: two layers}\label{sec:4}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/f1_two_layers.pdf}
\caption{The two layers: what an external observer can collect automatically, and what it structurally cannot.}\label{fig:1}
\end{figure*}

Behavioural provenance splits cleanly in two, and the split follows from what an observer can see rather than from an effort budget. \textbf{Layer 1, what happened and when}: activities, timings, tool and model calls, token counts, dataflow --- a framework callback receives run trees with inputs, outputs and parents, from which all of it follows mechanically, so \emph{this layer can be automated and the ecosystem has already automated it.} \textbf{Layer 2, why it happened}: the verdict, the confidence, the justification. \emph{This layer cannot be automated}, for the reason in Section~\ref{sec:1}. Our position follows: \textbf{consume Layer 1 where it exists, own Layer 2.} The vocabulary is framework-independent and only a thin adapter binds the lifecycle to a framework's events; the demonstrator is the bare-daemon case, with no agent framework at all, which is the strongest available evidence that the library is not welded to one runtime.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
tracker = ProvTracker(sid=session, agent_label="NL_Summarizer", sink=publish)

with tracker.activity("ToolCallActivity", "write the summary") as prov:
    prov.consumes(task)
    summary = llm.complete(ACT_SYSTEM, facts)
    prov.produces("ResponseEntity", summary.text, pid=pointer, derived_from=task)
    prov.note(**summary.provenance())          # model, prompt hash, tokens, latency

tracker.decide(                                 # the hand-written line
    verdict="REVISE",
    justification="Figures 4096 and 512 appear in the summary but not in the graph's "
                  "counts, so the text asserts something the record does not support.",
)
tracker.emit()
\end{lstlisting}

The wrappers supply exactly what is observable: type, label, start instant, measured duration, attribution, sequence position, and the failure case --- a step that raises is recorded as having run and failed, because a gap in a record is indistinguishable from a step that never ran. What must be declared is what is not observable. A wrapper sees that a function ran; it does not see which of the values in scope that function treated as its input, so lineage is stated through the scope and never inferred. We learned this the hard way: an earlier tracker inferred derivations from registration order, producing 74 confident and wrong edges out of 250. \texttt{wasInformedBy} is the one relation that \emph{is} automatic, because PROV defines it as derivable.

\textbf{\texttt{decide()} raises on a blank justification.} Every other field can be filled in by machinery; this one cannot, so the API refuses to let a caller skip it --- at the call site, where the reasoning still exists as a value. This has a consequence we did not anticipate (Section~\ref{sec:6.6}).

\textbf{Timing is measured, not merely stamped.} PROV records an activity as two instants, which invites reading both from the wall clock. That loses short steps: our host's clock advances in steps of roughly 15.6 ms, so two readings either side of a sub-millisecond operation return the \emph{same} value. Before we corrected this, \textbf{96 of 96 activities recorded a zero duration} --- a timeline from which nothing about where a run spent its time can be recovered. The start instant now comes from the wall clock, where it must be to compare across processes; the duration from a monotonic counter.

\section{The demonstrator}\label{sec:5}

A message bus, a producer replaying real yProv4ML records, four operational agents that do work and emit typed PROV about themselves, and two meta agents that consume \emph{that} and emit PROV in turn. There is \textbf{no agent framework}; the agents are bare asyncio daemons.

CAx & NL\_Summarizer & summarises a graph in natural language & LLM, computed fallback \\

The operational agents instantiate all six lifecycle steps; the meta agents instantiate a three-step subset, because they neither plan an approach nor answer a caller. We therefore phrase C3 as \textbf{a template with declared subsets}, not as uniform instantiation.

Two design decisions are visible in the provenance, and worth stating because the record is what makes them checkable. The store sits \emph{behind} the persist agent rather than on the bus: CA1 writes through the store's own API, and the bus carries a storage receipt --- a \texttt{yp:\allowbreak{}pid} and a content hash --- never a payload. And persistence happens \emph{after} consumption: CA1 holds each record, counts acknowledgements from three named consumers, and persists at quorum, with a timeout guard so a crashed subscriber cannot lose a record. The agent must say which path it took, and \texttt{yp:\allowbreak{}escalation\_\allowbreak{}reason} separates three: \emph{integrity}, \emph{incomplete-input} and \emph{consumption-unconfirmed} --- conditions that demand different operator responses. Every envelope on the bus is numbered, and an agent that finds a gap records an \texttt{INCOMPLETE} decision naming the missing envelopes rather than a well-formed record of a truncated input.

\subsection{Reflexivity, and the depth we claim}\label{sec:5.1}

Meta agents subscribe to \texttt{agent.\allowbreak{}lifecycle.\allowbreak{}>} and publish to \texttt{governance.\allowbreak{}<role>.\allowbreak{}<session>}. Nothing subscribes to \texttt{governance.\allowbreak{}*}, so the governance layer cannot re-trigger itself and \textbf{reflexivity in a run is one level deep}: the operational agents are governed; the governance layer is not.

That bound is a termination property rather than an oversight --- an agent that governs governance output emits a record which is itself governance output, and nothing in the subject structure stops the next pass. Publishing to a prefix nobody subscribes to makes the recursion impossible by construction, which is worth more than a depth counter someone can forget to check, and it is enforced as a test rather than documented as a caveat. Two things hold at that depth, both stronger than a governance table: the meta agents are fully instrumented, and \textbf{MC1 satisfies its own standard} --- applying MC1 to MC1's own output \textbf{failed} when first run, because the checker could not satisfy its own rule. It now passes, as does MC1 applied to MC3.

One container image serves all six roles and is rootless-friendly, so it converts cleanly to Apptainer for HPC. A second path runs the same agents over an in-process bus; CI runs both on every commit and holds each to the same structural comparison against the reference archive. They are not the same evidence --- the in-process bus awaits each handler inline in subscription order, exercising none of the distributed path's concurrency --- so we use it for isolation in measurement, never as evidence about ordering or throughput.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:6}

Every figure is regenerated from frozen archives under \texttt{experiments/\allowbreak{}} by \texttt{uv run python run\_\allowbreak{}local.\allowbreak{}py \&\& uv run python -m eval.\allowbreak{}evaluate}; none is transcribed by hand. Each archive carries per-file hashes and the commit it was frozen from, and refuses to freeze from a working tree with uncommitted changes. A CI matrix (Linux and Windows, Python 3.11 and 3.12) re-runs the pipeline and fails if any lineage edge of the emitted provenance changes without the change being declared; 239 tests pass. The reference run (\texttt{experiments/\allowbreak{}audit-reference}) is two input records through six agents, producing \textbf{24 PROV documents}.

\subsection{Conformance, coverage, and a declared standard}\label{sec:6.1}

Interoperability with W3C PROV is judged by an independent implementation. A single non-standard key at the document root made the reference \texttt{prov} parser reject \textbf{every one of 24 documents} whole --- the interoperability claim had been \emph{inverted}, not merely weak --- and after the fix all 24 are accepted and all 8 \texttt{yp:\allowbreak{}pid} pointers dereference from a fresh process. The run produces 96 activities and 324 relations, and the total is the wrong number to quote: \textbf{126 observed}, \textbf{72 derived} from PROV's definition of \texttt{wasInformedBy}, \textbf{30 declared}, and \textbf{96 attributed}, one per activity. That last category counts how many steps ran rather than how they were connected, so \textbf{the structural claim is 228, not 324.}

Two further measurements are easy to confuse, and only the second is a result. \textbf{Coverage: 24 of 24 decisions carry a justification} --- a conformance check on our own instrumentation, since the code that counts decision activities is the code that justifies them, so no run could report another number and the figure says nothing about whether a justification is \emph{informative}. \textbf{Conformance: 24 of 24 records satisfy the published shapes}, including the constraint that each agent decided something --- and this has an external judge, \texttt{pyshacl}~\cite{pyshacl} over RDF converted by the reference \texttt{prov} library. Neither the rule nor the engine is ours.

Applied to its own input rather than to our output, CA3 found \textbf{real completeness gaps in yProv4ML's published records}: 8 of 9 activities declare a start time with no end time, and entity typing coverage is 82.1\%.

The record therefore carries each energy figure with \texttt{yp:\allowbreak{}energy\_\allowbreak{}sampling\_\allowbreak{}period\_\allowbreak{}s}, \texttt{yp:\allowbreak{}energy\_\allowbreak{}source} and \texttt{yp:\allowbreak{}energy\_\allowbreak{}attributable}, so it states its period, what was observed, and that the figure is \textbf{not} an attribution to the step it is attached to. \textbf{Energy belongs at session granularity}, which is where Table~\ref{tab:4} measures it. \emph{A record that reports an energy figure without its sampling period and its source is reporting when the sampler last ticked, and how it guessed.}

\subsection{Model provenance}\label{sec:6.4}

Before this work the graph named no model, no sampling parameters, no prompt and no token counts, so the one step in the pipeline that was not deterministic was the one step the record could not account for. Every model-bearing step now carries the model, temperature, token limit, prompt identity, version and content hash, token counts, latency, finish reason, and a hash of the response (\texttt{experiments/\allowbreak{}audit-llm}: four calls, 2,071 prompt and 480 completion tokens). Three details generalise. \emph{The model a caller names is not the model that answers}: the record carries the requested alias \texttt{gpt-4o}, the snapshot that answered, \texttt{gpt-4o-2024-08-06}, and the provider's \texttt{system\_\allowbreak{}fingerprint} --- and the four calls of one run returned three distinct fingerprints. \emph{A prompt is apparatus and had to be routed like one}: a prompt registry with versions and hashes already existed and nothing imported it, because both LLM agents built prompts as inline f-strings; the fix was making the call site take a prompt object rather than a string, so a prompt cannot reach a model without its identity reaching the record. And \emph{an absence is recorded as an absence}: the computed fallback path stamps no model attributes at all, rather than zeros, because a zero token count is a measurement and on that path none was made.

\subsection{What the instrumentation costs}\label{sec:6.5}

Absolute timings are machine-specific --- a four-core laptop, CPython 3.11, with other processes on it. The transferable results are the ratios, the byte counts and the complexity class. A difference is reported as a result only if it reaches \textbf{3$\times$ the dispersion of its own samples}.

Figures are from \texttt{experiments/\allowbreak{}audit-reference/\allowbreak{}bench}, with a second occasion in \texttt{bench-previous}.

\textbf{Building the record is inside the noise; writing it is not always.} Each agent's step was timed 30 times per condition, interleaved, with provenance off, built but not written, and built and written. For all four operational agents, \emph{building} a complete typed record moved the step by less than its own run-to-run variance on both occasions (margins 0.0--0.5$\times$). Whether \emph{writing} it did depends on the agent and, for two of them, on the day.

\textbf{The field this paper argues for costs nothing we can resolve in time.} Over 40 interleaved rounds of 1,000 calls, the paired difference between recording an activity with and without a justification is \textbf{54 ns against a round-to-round spread of $\pm$726 ns}. In storage it costs bytes: 240--738 per justification in the reference records, \textbf{6.5\% of their serialised size}. The trade is \textbf{storage for explainability, not speed for explainability}.

Read the decomposition before believing the ratio. Explicit instrumentation is not expensive to \emph{compute}; persisting its output is, and persistence is a deployment decision. Both knobs are exposed and measured: transient mode is \textbf{$-$89\% of the time cost} for a byte-identical record, and metadata granularity \textbf{$-$37.6\% of the bytes} while retaining every justification. A mode that shed justifications would be a different product, not a cheaper one.

Two further results transfer. \textbf{The dominant per-step cost is rendering an instant as text} --- of a 4.97 \textmu{}s tracked step, 2.59 \textmu{}s is the timestamp, and formatting the value costs 1.8$\times$ reading the clock. And \textbf{the cost is linear, but was not when first measured}: the first sweep fitted an exponent of 1.468 because a duplicate check on declared derivations scanned a list, and a 10,000-activity record went from 1.72 s to 88 ms once that became an O(1) set; the exponents are now 1.09 for building a record and 1.13 for serialising it. That defect was invisible at the six activities every test uses and would have reached this paper as an unqualified linear claim.

\textbf{Which figures transfer.} Across two occasions the headline timings moved by 6--11\%, byte counts were identical, and exponents agreed within 2.1\%. Not every verdict held: for CA3 and CAx, building and writing went from 2.9$\times$ the dispersion to 5.0$\times$ and 4.6$\times$, resolved on one occasion and not the other, which is a result in neither direction. \textbf{Quote the bytes, the complexity class, the verdicts that held and the decomposition; quote the timings as orders of magnitude.} The 3$\times$ criterion is stricter than the one we began with, \emph{larger than the noise}, which treats any consistent bias as a finding, and a loaded machine supplies plenty. The project's own guard test caught it --- on a shared CI runner, timing the \textbf{same function twice} produced a 147.5 ns difference against 61.0 ns of dispersion and called it real. \textbf{Write the guard test that fails when your harness reports noise as a result.}

\subsection{Verdict reachability}\label{sec:6.6}

A verdict that only ever takes one value carries no information, so what has to be established is that the alternatives are \textbf{reachable}. The six agents have two verdicts each; the ordinary run reaches six of those twelve. Nine injected scenarios (\texttt{experiments/\allowbreak{}audit-reference/\allowbreak{}faults}) reach the other six, CA1's ESCALATE by each of its three reasons, and \texttt{INCOMPLETE}, which any stream-consuming agent records when an envelope goes missing. \textbf{All thirteen verdicts are observed.} Three of the scenarios are not faults at all, and that matters: CA2's ACCEPT, CAx's REVISE and CA3's OK are unreachable from the bundled sample data because the real records carry no numeric values to plot and declare start times without end times. So \emph{``CA2 always says REVISE''} was a finding about the \textbf{input}, not about CA2.

One row is a property rather than a result. \textbf{\texttt{mc1:\allowbreak{}FAIL} is unreachable from records this library produces}, because \texttt{decide()} refuses a blank justification at the call site. FAIL is reachable for records the library did not write --- exactly the case a compliance checker exists for, since it is pointed at agents you do not control. \emph{The instrumentation makes the violation unrepresentable, and the governance layer catches it in input that was not instrumented.} Every fault is injected into the input or the environment, \textbf{never into the check being tested}, so a verdict advertised and never observed fails the build.

\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:7}

\textbf{We measure that justifications exist and that their decision rule is externally unrecoverable; we do not measure whether they are \emph{good}.} This is the most significant limitation of the evaluation. Section~\ref{sec:6.1}'s coverage figure is a self-report by construction, and Section~\ref{sec:6.2} establishes unrecoverability, not quality. Section~\ref{sec:6.2}'s fact-level measure is also coarse in both directions: it counts a fact as seen wherever the same token appears, and it cannot see a rule that cites no checkable fact at all. Whether an operator handed one of these justifications acts correctly more often is an empirical question requiring a human study, and we have not run one. Fig.~\ref{fig:2} is an argument, not evidence.

\textbf{Reflexivity is one level deep}, and self-application is demonstrated as a property rather than as a running loop, so C2 carries that qualifier.

\textbf{A provenance system can over-report as easily as under-report.} An earlier version of our tracker asserted derivation from registration order and informed-by from recording order, producing exactly \emph{n}$-$1 edges for \emph{n} items regardless of what the agent did. The relations were corrected to declared-or-derived, and the correction changed the graph's \emph{topology} rather than only its size: the old chain asserted a single dependency spine where the real dataflow is a tree. We disclose this because it is what makes the counts in Section~\ref{sec:6.1} trustworthy, and because \textbf{relation counts are the easiest place in a provenance system for over-reporting to go unnoticed.}

\textbf{A model-bearing step is repeatable from its record, not bitwise reproducible.} The record carries what an auditor needs to re-run a call and what catches a forgotten version bump, but it cannot make a hosted model deterministic. Recording the resolved snapshot and fingerprint narrows the gap without closing it: three fingerprints in four calls of one run is a backend changing underneath a caller that changed nothing.

\textbf{Identifiers, transport, and resource bounds.} The \texttt{21.\allowbreak{}T11999} prefix is not registered and nothing resolves it globally, and identical payloads collapse to one identifier, so a pointer identifies \emph{content} rather than a storage event. NATS~\cite{nats} core is at-most-once with JetStream unconfigured, so a subscriber not running when a record is published never sees it --- a provenance bus that can lose provenance deserves a sentence in a provenance paper. Numbered envelopes turn a \emph{partial} loss into an \texttt{INCOMPLETE} record; a stream lost whole is still silent. And a tracker holds its whole record in memory until it emits (\textasciitilde{}1.3 KiB per activity), which is why a metadata-only granularity exists.

\textbf{Energy, on one machine.} The CPU counter covers the whole processor package, so background load reaches every window; interleaving shares it and the idle windows subtract it, but neither removes it. Memory energy is a model on this host and is excluded from the comparison rather than measured.

\textbf{Scale, a single producer, and our own baseline.} Two input records, 24 emitted documents, one producer; nothing in the agents is ML-specific, but that generality is argued from the code rather than demonstrated against a second producer. And we built Strategy A, so we could in principle have built it badly --- the mitigation is to grant it every structural fact the explicit instrumentation declares and report it as an upper bound, which is why Table~\ref{tab:2} concedes A everything except the one row the paper is about.

\section{Conclusion and future work}\label{sec:8}

The provenance community can describe what an agentic system did. With the automatic-capture tooling that dominates agent observability it cannot describe why --- and this is a structural property rather than a tooling deficiency. The facts a decision used may cross a step boundary; the rule that combined them is a local computation at the moment of decision, and no callback, span, or post-hoc query can retrieve what was never captured.

We made that argument measurable by building the strongest callback-level baseline we could and granting it every structural advantage: it reconstructs 96 of 96 activities, all 324 relations and 24 of 24 verdicts, and serialises to conformant W3C PROV-JSON. It sees 61\% of the facts the operational justifications cite, none of the values the agents computed in order to decide, and not one decision rule. We discharged the argument with a small explicit instrumentation layer --- a published \texttt{yp:\allowbreak{}} vocabulary with SHACL shapes, and an API whose decision call refuses a blank justification --- and demonstrated it across six heterogeneous agents in a running reflexive system, including the agents that govern the others. We measured the cost: building the record is inside every operational agent's run-to-run variance, the justifications are 6.5\% of the records' bytes, and where overhead resolves at all, at least 90\% of it is the write to disk. Measured over whole sessions, the energy cost does not resolve either; and per step it cannot be measured at all, which constrains how this community should record energy, and what a record must say about where its figure came from.

The honest summary of the trade is one sentence: \textbf{storage for explainability, not speed for explainability.}

Four extensions follow directly. \textbf{Actuation} is the largest: the meta layer today observes, judges and gates, and making it \emph{reconfigure} or \emph{activate} an operational agent requires a control-channel design with its own provenance --- an action taken on the basis of a justification should be derivable from it. \textbf{Bounded second-level reflexivity}, with the depth carried in the subject so recursion remains impossible by construction, would lift the one-level qualifier on C2. \textbf{A Layer-1 OpenTelemetry bridge} would let the automatic half of the record come from instrumentation a deployment already runs, leaving only the irreducible explicit part to the developer. And \textbf{a justification-quality study} is the missing evaluation: we have shown that a decision rule can only be captured and never retrieved, and not yet that capturing it makes an operator's decisions better.
