## E7 — Energy *(whole sessions)*

12 rounds; each round runs every condition for 97 whole six-agent sessions (a median 10 s window), plus an IDLE window of 20 s, interleaved with the starting condition rotated, after one whole round recorded and excluded as a warm-up. Energy is read from codecarbon 3.3.1's cumulative counters at each window's edges, sampling every 1 s.

| Component | Interface | Source | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPU | `windows_emi` | **measured** | the whole processor package, every process on the machine |
| RAM | codecarbon RAM model | **modelled** | a flat 10 W, whatever the memory does |

| B | 1.72 J | *in noise* (2.3×) | 1.32 J | *in noise* (1.5×) | 15.0 W | 114 ms |

The machine draws 3.2 W of CPU power idle; that is subtracted, round by round, to give the energy of the work itself.

### What this says

**Recording provenance changes the energy of a session by an amount this study cannot resolve.** No condition differs from OFF by 3× the spread of its own rounds, gross or above idle: a full session costs 1.43 J of CPU energy without provenance and 1.72 J with it.

**The medians point where E6 points.** Building the record without writing it (B-TRANS) moves a session's CPU energy by +5%; building and writing it (B) by +20%, alongside +9% in time per session. Neither difference resolves, so neither is a result; the ordering is the same as the timing study's, where the write is the cost.

**Measured and modelled are not added together.** The CPU figure is measured; the RAM figure is modelled, and at a flat 10 W it is 40% of the total a single sum would report, contributing nothing but elapsed time. It is published, labelled, and left out of every comparison above.

**The counter covers the whole processor package**, not this process. Background load is shared by every condition through interleaving and removed from each round by its own IDLE window, but it is not absent; a quiet machine would tighten every spread in this table.
